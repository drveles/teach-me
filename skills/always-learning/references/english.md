# English Coaching

Use an existing enabled English subject; do not create a duplicate. Enrollment follows [subject management](subject-management.md). Capture the learner's practical goal and preferred coaching intensity. Default to brief coaching during everyday tasks plus optional practice sessions. A diagnostic is evidence for the next exercise, not a certified language level.

## Everyday tasks

Complete the user's task in the same response. Select at most one meaningful or recurring error in the learner's own English, give a natural correction and one short explanation. Do not correct quoted text, code, deliberate informal wording, or every typo. Correct English needs no correction. Never make task completion depend on answering an exercise.

An original sentence is an attempt; a correction you supply is feedback, not successful retrieval. Save a recurring pattern only when it serves an existing objective. If no stored item fits, follow the item-creation step in [the teaching loop](teaching-loop.md). Do not turn every message into a new review item.

When the user says `just answer`, suppress coaching for that turn without pausing the subject. `Skip` ends the current exercise without a grade. `Pause` persists the subject's disabled state. Urgent tasks take priority over coaching.

## Dedicated practice

Ask one short production question before showing a model answer. Let the learner attempt an unfamiliar construction before explaining it. If they are stuck, offer a progressive hint, then a worked example; avoid repeated guessing. After the attempt, give specific corrective feedback and a natural version.

Select a useful difficulty: change the audience, tense, or situation rather than introducing obscure vocabulary. Interleave related constructions across exercises so the learner must choose the right pattern. Occasionally ask why a correction works or invite comparison with a known pattern; do not add a second simultaneous question.

For transfer, reuse a pattern in a new situation, such as moving from a colleague's request to a hotel booking. Avoid repeating the exact training sentence. Text-only performance does not establish pronunciation, listening ability, or speaking fluency.

## Evidence and review

Apply [the shared grading rubric](teaching-loop.md). A correct answer after a hint is `hard`; independent correct use is `good`; independent correct transfer to a new case is `easy`. Repeating a supplied answer is not independent recall. Never invent a response, confidence rating, or permanent mastery claim.

Revisit patterns after a delay using the stored schedule. Due dates are priorities when a relevant conversation occurs, not automatic notifications. The helper's 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, and 60-day intervals are a practical heuristic, not a schedule prescribed by the book.

## Sources

The learning design applies retrieval, spacing, interleaving, generation, elaboration, calibration, and transfer from the [authors' overview of Make It Stick](https://www.makeitstick.com/about-make-it-stick). The English exercises are applications of those principles, not claims that the book specifies an English curriculum.

Verify language explanations against appropriate references, such as [British Council grammar](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar) or [Cambridge English Grammar Today](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/). Prefer task-relevant sections over generic homepages when recording sources. When verification is unavailable, acknowledge the source gap rather than inventing a citation or verification date.
